Flipped 180° — face-inversion effect

Our visual system processes upright vs. inverted faces differently, changing which frequency band feels dominant.

Classic Oliva–Schyns hybrid image: frequencies at or below the shared cutoff come from the low-freq source, everything above comes from the high-freq source. A raw high-pass reconstruction naturally has much lower contrast than a low-pass one (no DC term), so it is auto-balanced to match before the boost slider is applied on top — 1.0× is balanced, higher favors the high-freq image, lower favors the low-freq image. Fourier sketching

A face becomes a pencil sketch. A Fourier high-pass filter finds the edges — the same frequency-domain masking as Frequency filtering, tuned here for clean line work — then a threshold turns them into crisp boundaries, and a classic color-dodge blend shades softly inside them.

Final pencil sketch

Grayscale → bilateral pre-smoothing (edge-preserving noise reduction) → FFT → a high-pass mask around the zero-frequency center (Gaussian rolloff or an ideal hard cutoff) → inverse FFT → Otsu or manual thresholding → color-dodge pencil shading confined to the thresholded edges. Auto-recomputes as you adjust any control.
